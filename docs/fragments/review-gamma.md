# Review-gamma trace activities

The source-railed trace activity rows use one format and evaluator. A printed bare `range` uses its geometric centre; when a source prints a central value and uncertainty, the point is that printed centre; `activity_range` is calculated from both printed gamma endpoints after applying the row's parent power. `x_source` follows the row's declared basis: one-cation rows count all oxide cations and replace the parent formula by its declared source units, while other component rows retain their parent-only expansion. Ga and In use the latter path as anchored to Bischof's Table 8 basis. Temperature selects the nearest source row. A composition screen adds a domain flag; Sn's explicit binary-slag rows are selected only when their binary screen is met.

Family screens compare the listed solvent-oxide family against declared oxides, count each absent family oxide, and exclude non-oxides such as S, Cl and F from the oxide distance. Other declared trace oxides remain part of the solvent context. The An-Di target is a printed major-oxide composition: compare it after renormalizing the declared target majors alone, excluding trace parents and non-oxides. Those components remain subject to their own trace checks. Result references identify the current family-distance and An-Di-basis revisions.

Both Ce activity equations use a pure solid Ce2O3 standard, while the gas parent is pure liquid Ce2O3(l). The sources do not name the polymorph; the evaluator takes the A phase, which is the stable form at 1873 K below the A→H transition at 2392 K. That choice is an inference, not a source statement. Equality of chemical potentials gives `a_l = a_A exp(-[G°_Ce2O3(l)(T)-G°_A(T)]/(RT))`. The evaluator now computes this direct Gibbs difference at each call temperature, using the shipped liquid-parent row and the Konings et al. (2014) phase-A heat-capacity function. The phase path includes A→H (28±8 kJ/mol at 2392 K), H→X (19±5 kJ/mol at 2406 K), and fusion (85±10 kJ/mol at 2512 K). The heat-capacity residual beyond the transition sum is 1.533, 0.843, and 0.199 kJ/mol at 1700, 1873, and 2100 K; the corresponding total ΔG values are 42.684, 32.749, and 19.974 kJ/mol. At 1873 K the conversion factor remains 0.12209. The positive gap makes liquid-standard activity smaller than stable-solid activity. The fusion enthalpy is estimated from La2O3 analogue entropies, not directly measured for Ce2O3. Each gamma band combines the equation's ln-gamma closure RMS with the three transition-enthalpy uncertainties in quadrature in log space. The central activity changes off 1873 K, while the relative band width remains the same because the same uncertainty terms are propagated about the corrected center. Chen is explicitly selected whenever Al2O3 is present and was measured at 5 wt% MgO and 10 wt% Ce2O3; Zhao is selected only at zero Al2O3. Even trace Al2O3 therefore switches to the Chen law and is flagged below its Table 2 range. Both equations are single-temperature screens for the source families at 1873 K and `pO2=4.76×10⁻¹⁶ atm` under graphite/pure-CO C/CO conditions; the evaluator flags temperature, oxygen-potential, and composition-range departures.

Ni and Co now use this shared evaluator while retaining their source-specific domain flags and result types. The simple-oxide fraction path preserves the original `n/sum(n)` arithmetic; Co retains its reported MgO increase note above 20 wt%.

Wood & Wade (2013), pp. 916–918, supplies the W and Pb rows. The Pb value is for the analyzed BW1102 silica-saturated melt; no uncertainty for its activity coefficient is stated, so its band is the point 0.22. The W endpoints are inferred from Fig. 4 contour labels and remain marked `contour-read`; the full 0.001–0.05 range is surfaced as its band. The default liquid PbO and WO3 parent rows cover 1923 K and use matching pure-liquid component standards, with one parent formula unit per source component.

Li et al. (2020) Table 2 gives an assessed La2O3-SiO2 binary dilute limit, `log10 γ∞ = -8130.0768/T + 0.6122121`, derived from the alternating Redlich–Kister parameter sums using the paper's `R = 8.314`. The basis is inferred as one La2O3 formula unit per RK component mole, matching the runtime formula-unit mole fraction and `k=1`. At 1873 K, `γ∞ = 1.8687×10⁻⁴`. This provisional phase-diagram fit to Toropov (1961) and Bondar (1982) is not a measured activity; Table 2 states no parameter uncertainty, so the point is the band. The operational 1773–1873 K window follows liquid-region assessment work at 1500–1600 °C rather than stated parameter-fit bounds. The evaluated number is the SiO2-edge dilute limit. In Li et al.'s Kohler ternary model with added `Gᴱ = -450000 x_La2O3 x_Al2O3 x_SiO2²`, γ∞ depends on the Al2O3:SiO2 ratio: at 1873 K it is about `9.8×10⁻⁶` at `x_SiO2=5/6` (19 times below the binary value) and `1.5×10⁻³` in pure Al2O3 (8 times above). The runtime row therefore flags any Al2O3 as outside the binary SiO2 edge. No CaO/MgO/FeO interaction is modeled, so the bias direction for those basic-melt extrapolations is unknown. For Al2O3-bearing melts, the bias direction depends on the solvent ratio.

Wood & Wade's inferred NiO range of 1.5–3 at 1923 K overlaps the existing Holzheid Ni point of 2.70 and reported spread of 0.52. The Holzheid temperature window ends at 1873.15 K, so this is a value-level cross-check outside that window, not same-domain validation; it is not an additional Ni row.

Sn values use Sossi & Fegley (2018), Table 2, which cites Takeda & Yazawa (1989) for the FCS band; the binary range is from Takeda et al. (1990). The standard state is liquid SnO, matching the NG-1848 parent. The NASA/Glenn card cites Gurvich (1991) and covers 1250–2000 K, including 1573 K; only the table's 1200–1250 K segment is a constant-Cp continuation. I found no same-temperature, independently parameterized pure-liquid SnO Gibbs function to quantify a standard offset, so the available evidence does not show a Ga/In-like continuation mismatch at the Sn measurement temperature.

I also checked the SnO(l) function compiled by Lamoreaux, Hildenbrand & Brewer (1987), *Journal of Physical and Chemical Reference Data* 16, DOI 10.1063/1.555799. It covers the same 1250–2000 K interval but is a selected compilation of high-temperature oxide data, not an independently parameterized comparator; I found no separate source-grounded SnO(l) value at 1573 K from which to calculate an offset.

Ga and In use the direct liquid-standard KEMS results in Bischof et al. (2023), Section 4.4.4: γ(GaO1.5)=0.036±0.006 at 1700 K and γ(InO1.5)=0.017±0.012 at 1674 K. The point is the printed central value, with the printed endpoints retained as a band. Both rows leave the fusion block empty because these coefficients are already for liquid MO1.5. Table 9's linear coefficients are not used as melt activities. Bischof's Table 8 `x` values come from LA-ICP-MS and are the MO1.5 formula-unit mole fraction: x(GaO1.5)=0.0172 for 429 ppm Ga (using the 695 ppm 2_low value for 429 ppm in 1_low) and x(InO1.5)=6.9×10⁻⁵ for the 0.0065–0.05 ppm 1_low surface range. These Table 8 fractions are not the analytical-ppm column converted to an ordinary bulk fraction, and that column is not used to validate the Table 8 basis. For openimcc callers, runtime `x_source` is computed from their melt composition on the same formula-unit MO1.5 basis by expanding each M2O3 parent into two MO1.5 units.

## Ga/In parent standard

Bischof's pure-oxide equilibrium constants are pressure-derived KEMS results for Ga2O3(s) and In2O3(s), reported in Bischof et al. (2023), *Calphad* 80, 102507, DOI 10.1016/j.calphad.2022.102507, Section 3.2, Eqs. (18)–(21), Figures 7–8, and the article's supplementary figure data. Calphad Eq. 19 sets the pressure standard to p0=10^5 Pa. Its third-law reaction enthalpies separately use FactSage Gibbs-energy functions (Section 3.2, Eqs. (27)–(30)): Fig. 9 identifies the Ga reference data as Chaplygin (1975), Hultgren et al. (1973), and Medvedev et al. (1981); Fig. 10 identifies the In set as Barin (1995), Chaplygin (1975), Hultgren et al. (1973), and Medvedev et al. (1981). Those database-derived third-law values are not the measured K used here. The 2023 GCA paper converts the empirical solid-oxide KEMS constants to liquid MO1.5 using the fusion correction in Eq. (18), then reports the liquid-standard fits in Eqs. (20) and (21). These pressure-derived K fits are used at p0=10^5 Pa. The opt-in table reconstructs parent Gibbs functions with the shipped JANAF gas functions. This measured KEMS basis is better founded in the 1550–1740 K experimental window than the engine's NASA-Glenn constant-Cp supercooled-liquid continuation.

The opt-in `condensate-ga-in-kems-candidate.csv` fits the parent liquid Gibbs functions from those liquid component constants. For component KEMS fit `ln K = -A/T + B`, the table uses

```text
ln K(M2O3,l), p0=1e5 Pa = 2 * (-A/T + B)
G°(M2O3,l) = 2 G°(M,g) + 1.5 G°(O2,g) + R T ln K(M2O3,l)
```

The parent equation is fitted over 1550–1740 K only. Rows outside that interval retain functions copied from `condensate-default-v1.csv`. The CSV source-label comment records the conversion. The default condensate table and selected defaults are unchanged. The candidate table's generated Gibbs coefficients are a fit of the published KEMS constants, not a replacement for the published table or a default selection.

For composition-derived Ga/In gas activities on the default path, openimcc converts the Bischof KEMS liquid activity to the NASA liquid parent standard used by the unchanged default condensate table. Equality of chemical potentials gives

```text
a_NASA = a_KEMS * exp((G_KEMS - G_NASA)/(R*T))
       = a_KEMS * exp(ln K_Bischof - ln K_NASA)
```

For `M2O3(l) = 2M(g) + 3/2O2(g)`, the second equality follows from `ln K° = (G_parent - G_products)/(R*T)`. The ratio is applied to the review-γ parent activity before evaluating gas pressures. At 1674 K, the In ratio is 0.00112583. The notice reports the applied `a_NASA/a_KEMS` ratio and whether it lies inside the measured fit or is extrapolated. The same published two-parameter `ln K` relation is used outside 1550–1740 K. A selected row with `Ref=BISCHOF-KEMS-2023` already uses the KEMS parent standard and bypasses conversion.

## Bischof S1 source-reproduction check

The earlier Table 9 activity with analytical ppm gave the prior `p/p(S1)` ratios below. The closure uses Table 8 `x` (Ga 0.0172; In 6.9×10⁻⁵), each S1 row's own γ (Ga 0.0632, 0.0285, 0.0248, 0.0246; In 0.0527, 0.0377, 0.0301, 0.0254), activity `(γ x)^2`, and the opt-in parent. Bischof et al. (2023), §3.2, p. 4, Eqs. 22–23 derive γ from the same measured melt pressures and `x` used in these S1 rows. Substituting that γ into the pressure relation reduces the row-wise pressure ratio to `K_engine/K_source` with both constants written per MO1.5 (MO1.5 → M(g) + ¾O2). For constants written per M2O3 (M2O3 → 2M(g) + 3/2 O2), the ratio is `sqrt(K_engine/K_source)`: with `γ = p_obs·pO2^(3/4)/(K_source,MO1.5·x)` and `a(M2O3) = (γx)^2`, `p_pred = sqrt(K_engine,M2O3)·γx/pO2^(3/4) = p_obs·sqrt(K_engine,M2O3)/K_source,MO1.5`. This is a source-reproduction check of the standard constants, not independent validation of melt activity. For the same dilute An-Di-like melt at 1550, 1674 and 1740 K, monatomic Ga(g) and In(g) default NASA-parent and opt-in KEMS-parent pressures differ by at most 2.86×10⁻⁶ relative; this is the residual between the candidate's five-coefficient Gibbs fit and the published two-parameter `ln K` form. The `n_gas=1` Ga2O and In2O channels can have about twice that relative residual because pressure scales with the parent activity to the power `1/n_gas`. The earlier and S1 columns use different source compositions, so the latter ratios reproduce the Table 8/S1 source constants:

These closures use Table 8's measured component fractions; the analytical-ppm column is not substituted for those `x` values. Runtime callers instead supply a melt composition, from which openimcc computes the same formula-unit `x_source` basis.

Cu, GeO2, As and Bi ranges are from the cited primary melt studies as summarized in Sossi & Fegley (2018), Table 2. The Cu family text is a flag, not the temperature selector. The As parent remains described as the engine's tier-4 liquid-from-solid estimate, not as independently measured pure liquid As2O3. GeO stays inactive; V now has the provisional Norris row documented below.

Norris's V, Ag, Sb, and Tl rows use the Table 4.16 mean as the point and its printed min–max as the band at 1923 K. These are inferred from metal–silicate partitioning with the Kf + γ-in-Fe model, not measured activities. The source's Section 4.11.2 says its activity coefficients are accurate within an order of magnitude; this limitation is carried separately and does not widen the printed bands. The evaluator flags compositions outside the source's ADF+FeO family. V assumes V3+ because no per-run valence was measured. Norris §4.6 says fO2 was not tabulated and was unconstrained, but was believed near Fe–FeO / IW because elemental Fe and FeO were both present. No Table 4.17 composition terms are applied: it reports R²=0.961 for V, 0.900 for Ag, and 0.807 for Tl, which can support a future refinement; the Sb regression failed. The Ag engine parent is P672-Ag2O-LFS, a liquid-from-solid construction with a hypothetical 500 K fusion anchor, while Norris's γ references Assal et al. (1997) liquid AgO0.5 (ln Kf at 1923 K = −6.493). The Sb engine parent is P672-Sb2O3-LFS, with insufficiently known fusion enthalpy and a 928 K marker; Norris's γ references Barin (1973) liquid Sb2O3 extrapolated past its 1729 K boiling point. For Tl, the shipped liquid parent at 1923 K uses Pankratz's constant-Cp extrapolation above 1000 K (`P672-Tl2O-l-CP-EXT-1000`), while the γ uses Lamoreaux et al. (1987) liquid Tl2O with one-cation ln Kf=1.67 at 1923 K. Norris §4.11.1 calls the Miedema γ-zero estimate an upper estimate for Bi, Cd, and Tl; the high direction of the inferred silicate γ follows Eq. 4.1, where it is proportional to the metal γ.

Sossi et al.'s RbO0.5 Table 4 values are nonmonotonic: 1.3×10⁻⁴ at 1673.15 K, 7.4×10⁻⁵ at 1773.15 K, and 9.0×10⁻⁵ at 1823.15 K. The range row uses their min–max and the observed center-temperature value as its point; it does not fit a temperature trend. The source reaction RbO0.5(l)=Rb(g)+¼O2 doubles to Rb2O(l)=2Rb(g)+½O2, matching the parent with k=2. With the open-evaporation Hertz–Knudsen relation and 0<αe≤1, setting αe=1 makes the apparent coefficient a lower bound when the measured flux and other terms are fixed. Sossi §4.6 says Table 4 uncertainties are fit precision only and exclude αe, which is close to unity within a factor of 2–3; some inverted Table 4 αe values exceed 1 (Zn 1.7–2.2) as inversion artifacts. FCMAS-external melts raise the family flag.

## Norris Table 4.16 cross-checks

These 1923 K means are method cross-checks against existing rows; they do not change those row sources. Norris reports Ga 1.1 versus the existing Bischof KEMS value 0.036 at 1700 K (about 30× higher), and GeO2 0.0037 versus the existing 0.24–1.5 range at 1573 K (about 65–405× lower). These are method disagreements. The other values are closer: In 0.030 versus 0.017 at 1674 K, Pb 0.23 versus 0.22 at 1923 K, Bi 0.46 versus 0.3–0.5 at 1573 K, W 0.064 versus 0.001–0.05 at 1923 K, and CuO0.5 4.7 versus 3.5 at 1923 K. Temperature and melt-family differences remain for the cross-temperature comparisons. Existing primary source rows, including selected KEMS rows, stay unchanged.
